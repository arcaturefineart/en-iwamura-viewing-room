Neo Jomon: Matryoshka, 2025 / Glazed ceramic / 15 ¾ × 10 ¼ × 10 ¼ in

Born in Kyoto, 1988. Lives and works in Shigaraki, a historic center of Japanese ceramics.

Practice

Ma

The work turns on Ma: the interval between objects, between people, and between a form and the room that holds it.

Iwamura builds by hand. Raked lines move across the surface. The expressions stay quiet. Ceramic tradition and contemporary visual culture share the same object.

Recent sculpture has moved from floor-based figures into wall-hanging masks and multi-part works, following an interest in perception, play, and the emotional experience of space.

Biography

Born in Kyoto, Japan, in 1988, En Iwamura lives and works in Shigaraki, a historic center of Japanese ceramics. Working across ceramic sculpture, painting, and drawing, he explores Ma: the intervals and relationships between objects, people, and the spaces they inhabit. His hand-built forms pair rhythmic, raked surfaces with quiet expressions, opening a dialogue between ancient ceramic traditions and contemporary visual culture.

Iwamura earned a BFA and MFA from Kanazawa College of Art and Craft and a second MFA from Clemson University. His residencies include the Archie Bray Foundation, Jingdezhen Ceramic Institute, and Shigaraki Ceramic Cultural Park. Recent work has expanded from floor-based figures into wall-hanging masks and multi-part sculptures, extending his interest in perception, play, and the emotional experience of space.
